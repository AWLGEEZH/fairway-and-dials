# Tee Box: pre-prototype plan (Nov 2026 – Jan 2027)

What has to be settled before money goes to a factory for Tee Box prototypes. Covers the next quarter and ends at a go/no-go for ordering 3–5 samples. Companion to `ROADMAP.md` (release order) and `PLAN.md` (site and shop).

Working premise: Fairway & Dials launches as a small-batch, pre-order microbrand (100 units) on a mechanical movement. Scaling to a Mr. Eatwell-sized business (800–1,200 units a year across a catalog plus a club/corporate channel) is a year-three question, not a gate for the first prototype.

## M1: Design freeze (by Nov 15)

Lock these, in `designs/dials/` and a new `designs/tech-pack/`:

- Dial: tee markers, ball at 12, printed cup at 6, full wordmark under 12, "Tee Box" beneath, "Automatic" above 6. Confirm print colors as Pantone or RAL, not screen hex.
- Hands: hour and minute shapes, lume color (C3 vs BGW9), flag on the seconds hand. Hand lengths relative to the dial diameter.
- Case: diameter (decided at M2), lug-to-lug, thickness, lug width (20 mm), crystal (double-domed sapphire), caseback (solid, engraved), crown (push-pull or screw-down), water resistance target (100 m).
- Movement: NH38 (no date) for the first run, per `ROADMAP.md`. Keep the 9039 as the fallback if the quotes favor it.
- Strap: default strap and one alternate.
- Differentiation check against the Vaer G2 Fairway: list the three details Vaer did not do (cup at 6, flag-in-cup at :30, sandwich dial) and make sure each is visible in the hero photo.

## M2: Case fit check (by Nov 30)

- Export the case profile as STL from the design files.
- Print at 38 and 40 mm through a Seattle print shop or a $30 online print service.
- Wear each shell on a strap for a few days. Note lug overhang, thickness, how it sits under a glove and a sleeve.
- Record the chosen size in the Decisions table.

## M3: Bench skills (by Dec 15)

- Two kit builds, 38–39 mm field case, NH35/NH38, from a vendor that ships a case with similar specs to the target.
- Dust protocol every build: clean mat, blower, finger cots, Rodico, inspect through the crystal under angled light before closing.
- Buy a timegrapher (Weishi 1000 or similar, ~$150). Regulate both builds and log the result.
- Order a hand-setting tool set and a movement holder if the kit did not include them.

## M4: Dial mule (by Dec 31)

- Get the Tee Box dial printed at real size. Options: a dial-printing service, or a local pad or UV printer. Two copies.
- Fit it in one of the M3 kit cases with the planned hand set.
- Photograph it: wrist shot, macro of the dial, flag in the cup at :30, on a tee box. These are the waitlist images.
- Hand the mule to two or three people who were not involved and ask what they notice first. If the answer is "a golf watch" before "a nice watch," revisit the balance.

## M5: Demand signal (by Jan 10)

- Waitlist page on fairwayanddials.com with the mule photos and a one-paragraph pitch; price range stated ($450–650).
- Push to the eBay buyer list, Instagram, and r/Watches. One post each, no paid spend yet.
- Target: 150 signups. Fewer is not a stop, but it changes the first-run size.
- Read the comments as carefully as the signup count.

## M6: Supplier shortlist (by Jan 20)

- Write the tech pack from the M1 spec: drawings with tolerances, dial artwork with print spec, hand spec, movement, packaging.
- Send to three assemblers. Candidates: a sourcing agent that works with microbrands, plus two direct factories (Shenzhen or Dongguan case makers that other PNW microbrands use; ask Typsim or the Seattle watch meetup for names).
- Ask for: sample cost and lead time, production MOQ and unit cost at 100 and 300, payment terms, what QC they do before shipping.

## M7: Go / no-go (by Jan 31)

Go if all three hold:

1. Sample quotes land under $1,000 per prototype and 16 weeks or less.
2. Waitlist shows real interest (signups plus comment quality).
3. Unit cost at 100 supports a $450–650 price with 35–40% net after fees, shipping, and warranty.

If go: place the sample PO, open a `prototype` label in issues, and write the reviewer outreach list (Seattle first: Zinski, Two Broke Watch Snobs; then two or three mid-size YouTube channels).

If no-go: record why in `PLAN.md` and decide whether the fix is price, design, or audience.
